01 / INTRODUCTION

What is Linear Regression?

Linear regression is a statistical and machine-learning method used to estimate the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables. It fits a line that minimises the sum of squared prediction errors in ordinary least-squares regression.

01

Understand

Identify how an input variable is associated with an outcome.

02

Estimate

Fit a mathematical equation to observed data.

03

Predict

Estimate an outcome for new inputs within a sensible range.

Key idea: A fitted line describes a pattern in observed data. It does not automatically prove that one variable causes another.
02 / MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION

The mathematics behind the model

Simple linear regression uses one input variable to estimate one numerical output.

The regression equation

ŷ = b₀ + b₁x
SymbolMeaning
xIndependent or input variable
ŷPredicted value of the output
b₀Intercept: predicted output when x = 0
b₁Slope: predicted change in output for a one-unit increase in x

Calculating the slope

b₁ = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) / Σ(x − x̄)²

The slope measures the direction and size of the fitted linear relationship.

Calculating the intercept

b₀ = ȳ − b₁x̄

The intercept makes the fitted line pass through the point of the sample means, (x̄, ȳ).

How does the model choose its line?

SSE = Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)²

Ordinary least squares selects the line that minimises the sum of squared residuals. A residual is the observed value minus the predicted value.

Residual = yᵢ − ŷᵢ
03 / STATISTICS & EVALUATION

How do we evaluate a regression model?

Model evaluation helps us understand fit, error and the strength of the observed relationship.

Descriptive statistics

Mean: Average of the observed values.

x̄ = Σxᵢ / n

Variance: Measures how spread out observations are.

s² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n − 1)

Standard deviation: Square root of variance.

Correlation

r = Cov(X,Y) / (sₓsᵧ)

Pearson's correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear association. It ranges from −1 to +1.

Correlation alone does not establish causation.

Coefficient of determination

R² = 1 − SSE / SST

Describes the proportion of outcome variation explained by the fitted model on the evaluated data, when the usual intercept model and definitions apply.

Prediction error

MAE = mean(|y − ŷ|)
MSE = mean((y − ŷ)²)
RMSE = √MSE

Smaller errors generally indicate closer predictions on the evaluated dataset. Compare metrics on the same outcome scale and test set.

Important distinction: A high R² does not guarantee reliable future predictions. Check residual patterns, outliers, assumptions and performance on unseen data.
04 / PRACTICAL CASE STUDY

Can study hours predict exam marks?

Consider this illustrative dataset. It demonstrates how a regression equation can turn a simple pattern into an estimate. These are example values, not actual student records.

StudentStudy hours (x)Exam marks (y)
A135
B240
C350
D455
E565

Step 1: Calculate the means

x̄ = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5) / 5 = 3
ȳ = (35 + 40 + 50 + 55 + 65) / 5 = 49

Step 2: Calculate the slope

Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) = 75
Σ(x − x̄)² = 10
b₁ = 75 / 10 = 7.5

Step 3: Calculate the intercept

b₀ = 49 − (7.5 × 3) = 26.5

Step 4: Write the final regression equation

ŷ = 26.5 + 7.5x

Step 5: Predict marks for 6 study hours

ŷ = 26.5 + (7.5 × 6) = 71.5
Interpretation: The model estimates 71.5 marks for six study hours. This is a mathematical estimate, not a guarantee of a student's actual marks.

05 / MACHINE-LEARNING WORKFLOW

From raw data to prediction

A reliable analysis requires more than simply drawing a line.

1. Define the problem

Choose a numerical outcome you want to estimate.

2. Collect and clean data

Handle missing values, inconsistent units and incorrect records.

3. Explore the dataset

Use summaries, scatter plots and correlations to understand patterns.

4. Split the data

Keep a test dataset separate to evaluate predictions on unseen observations.

5. Train the model

Estimate the regression coefficients using training data.

6. Evaluate and interpret

Review MAE, RMSE, R², residuals and the assumptions relevant to your task.

06 / REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS

Where is linear regression used?

It is especially useful when a numerical outcome has a meaningful approximately linear relationship with its predictors.

Business & sales

Estimate sales using advertising spend, pricing or historical trends.

Real estate

Estimate house prices using area, location features and property characteristics.

Education

Study the association between study time, attendance and examination results.

Healthcare research

Analyse relationships between numerical measurements and clinical outcomes, with appropriate expert oversight.

Finance & operations

Estimate costs, demand or numerical business indicators using relevant predictors.

Environmental analysis

Explore trends in temperature, pollution measurements or energy consumption.

07 / CRITICAL THINKING

Advantages and limitations

Advantages

✓ Easy to understand and communicate.

✓ Fast to train on many datasets.

✓ Coefficients help explain fitted relationships.

✓ Provides a useful baseline for predictive modelling.

Limitations

✕ May perform poorly for nonlinear patterns.

✕ Sensitive to influential outliers.

✕ Correlated predictors can make coefficients unstable.

✕ Extrapolation can be unreliable.

✕ Statistical assumptions matter for inference and uncertainty estimates.

Final conclusion

Linear regression connects mathematics, statistics and practical prediction. By estimating a line of best fit, we can describe relationships, quantify prediction errors and build a useful starting point for data-driven decisions. Its value depends on data quality, suitable assumptions and honest evaluation.
